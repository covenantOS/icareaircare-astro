<h2>Low charge means a hole, not an empty tank</h2>

<p>Homeowners still ask us to "put some Freon in it." R-410A and the newer A2L blends are not motor oil. They do not burn off. If gauges show a light charge, refrigerant left the sealed system through a leak. Topping it off without finding the hole buys you a few weeks of cooling and a second bill. Venting refrigerant to hide a leak is also an EPA problem. Tim holds Section 608 Universal for a reason.</p>

<p>The evaporator coil is one of the most common leak sites we confirm in Tampa Bay. It lives wet. It lives in a cabinet full of household vapors. Aluminum microchannel and tube-and-fin coils both fail here, just on slightly different timelines.</p>

<blockquote><strong>Tim's tip:</strong> if a previous company added refrigerant and did not show you a leak location, a nitrogen test, or a dye photo, treat that visit as a delay, not a repair. Ask for the micron vacuum reading and the weighed-in charge against the nameplate on the next call.</blockquote>

<h2>What an evaporator coil leak looks like from the living room</h2>

<p>You will not see a puddle of refrigerant. You will see the system behave like it is starving.</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Ice, then cooling, then ice again.</strong> Change the filter, thaw with the fan on, get a day or two of cold air, then frost returns on the suction line. Airflow problems do not reset on that schedule. Charge problems do. Our <a href="/blogs/frozen-evaporator-coil-tampa/">frozen coil guide</a> covers the thaw steps. This page is what happens after the second freeze.</li>
<li><strong>Hissing or bubbling at the air handler.</strong> Not always audible. When it is, it is a locating clue, not a DIY invitation.</li>
<li><strong>Oil staining at a braze or a return bend.</strong> Refrigerant leaves as gas. Oil often leaves a dirty film that points at the joint.</li>
<li><strong>Warm supply air with the outdoor unit running hard.</strong> Superheat climbs. Subcooling falls. Those are meter readings, not guesses from the driveway.</li>
<li><strong>A hiss at a Schrader valve on a newer home.</strong> Builder-grade valves weep. That leak is on the coil assembly even if the fin pack looks clean.</li>
</ul>

<p>A dirty coil can freeze too. The difference is persistence. A wash and a filter hold when dirt was the restriction. They do not hold when the circuit is leaking. If you have not ruled dirt out, start with the <a href="/blogs/dirty-evaporator-coil-tampa/">dirty evaporator coil</a> post, then come back here.</p>

<h2>Why Florida coils leak: formicary, vibration, and new-house chemistry</h2>

<p>Formicary corrosion, also called ant-nest corrosion, eats microscopic tunnels through aluminum from the inside of the tube. Organic acids from paint, cabinetry, adhesives, and cleaning products mix with the condensate that never really dries off a Florida coil. Newer interiors in Epperson, Mirada, and Wiregrass put more of those vapors in the return air in the first few years. Homes closer to the bay see extra corrosion load on outdoor copper. Indoor coils still fail inland. The mechanism is the wet coil plus the chemistry, not salt spray on Foamflower Blvd.</p>

<p>The rest of the list is mechanical. Vibration at a poorly supported line set. A factory braze that was always thin. A coil that got stabbed during a DIY "thaw" with a screwdriver. We still see that last one more than I would like.</p>

<p>R-22 systems leaking at the coil are a replacement conversation more than a repair conversation. R-410A systems in the 8 to 12 year window are the ones where we still run the numbers both ways.</p>

<h2>How we find the leak instead of guessing</h2>

<p>Electronic sniffers, UV dye, and nitrogen pressure testing are the three tools that actually locate a hole. Dye is slow and messy if someone already dumped a bottle in last summer. Nitrogen with a standing pressure test tells you whether the circuit holds. A bubble solution at joints catches the obvious weeps. We do not "add two pounds and see how you feel in August."</p>

<p>Once the leak is in the coil body itself, especially formicary, a spot repair on aluminum almost never lasts. The next pinhole opens a few inches away. Coil replacement, a deep vacuum to 500 microns, and a weighed charge against the data plate is the repair that stays done. If the outdoor condenser is the same age and tired, we put a <a href="/blogs/hvac-replacement-cost-tampa-bay/">full changeout</a> next to the coil-only number so you can see both columns. Age times repair cost versus $5,000 is the same rule we use on compressors.</p>
