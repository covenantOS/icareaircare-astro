<h2>When should a Wesley Chapel home switch from AC to heat?</h2>

<p>Do not pick a date on the calendar. October in Wesley Chapel still produces warm afternoons, and a cold front can make the house feel cold before sunrise. Tampa International's October normal is a high of 85.6°F and a low of 69.2°F, with about 2.34 inches of rain for the month. That is the National Weather Service 1991-2020 normal. Wesley Chapel sits inland of the airport, so nights there run cooler than the Tampa reading. If the house is uncomfortable in the morning, test the heat. If the afternoon climbs back through the 80s, put the system back on cool. Most weeks here need both.</p>

<p>Auto mode handles that swing if the gap between setpoints is wide. Heat near 68 and cool near 76 lets the equipment sit idle in the middle of the range. Heat at 72 and cool at 74 makes a heat pump flip modes on the same afternoon. That extra shifting wears the reversing valve, and the heating cycle gives back humidity the cooling cycle just removed.</p>

<p>Older houses in Seven Oaks and Meadow Pointe often still have a round dial on the wall. Newer houses in Epperson and Mirada have a digital thermostat with a Heat, Cool, and Auto button. The rule is the same on both. You are not shutting the air conditioner off for the season. You are letting the system choose, with enough space between the two setpoints that it does not fight itself.</p>

<h2>Why does the house feel damp when the thermostat is already satisfied?</h2>

<p>This shows up every October in Seven Oaks, Meadow Pointe, and Wiregrass. The number on the thermostat is fine. The rooms still feel clammy.</p>

<p>Runtimes got shorter. Afternoons are milder than July, so the air conditioner reaches the setpoint and shuts off. Dew points do not drop as fast as the temperature. Less runtime means less water pulled out of the air. A house that felt dry at 74 in August can feel sticky at 74 in October.</p>

<p>Look at the fan setting before you drop the temperature. Leave it on Auto. Fan On keeps the blower spinning between cycles, and that blower lifts water off a wet coil and sends it back into the rooms. If a humidity reading in the house stays above 60 percent for several days, the filter, the coil, and the drain are the next things to check. That is an <a href="/services/ac-repair-tampa/">air conditioner repair</a> problem. Making the house colder will not dry it out.</p>

<p>A clogged condensate drain makes this worse. The coil is still making water on the afternoons that do run, and if the line is slow the pan stays wet between cycles. Musty air at the first start of the day is often that pan, not a mysterious fall mold event. Our note on <a href="/blogs/ac-leaking-water-tampa/">AC water leaks</a> covers the float switch and the algae plug. October is when people notice it, because the system is no longer running long enough to hide a weak drain.</p>

<h2>Should you shut the air conditioner off for the fall?</h2>

<p>Keep it available. Shutting the system off in October leaves the next 85-degree afternoon with no cooling, and it leaves the heat side untested. <a href="/service-areas/wesley-chapel-ac-repair/">AC repair in Wesley Chapel</a> does not stop because the calendar says fall. Capacitors, contactors, and algae in the drain still fail after a summer of long runtimes.</p>

<p>Outdoor units behind the hedges in Meadow Pointe and Seven Oaks are often dirtier now than they were in June, because the shrubs kept growing while the unit ran. Restricted air across the coil raises head pressure. That is a regular reason a capacitor or a fan motor fails on a mild week, right when the homeowner thought the hard season was over. Two feet of open space around the cabinet matters more than a perfect mow.</p>

<p>What can change is how you read a normal cycle. A system that reaches the setpoint and then rests is doing the job. A system that runs and never catches up, blows warm air, or starts and stops every few minutes needs air conditioner repair. The second pattern is the one to call about. The first one is October weather.</p>

<h2>What should the first heat run do?</h2>

<p>Pick a mild afternoon this month and run the heat for 15 minutes. Stay in the house while it runs. You want warm air at a supply vent, and you want the outdoor unit running.</p>

<p>Most homes here do not have a gas furnace. They have a heat pump. The outdoor unit that cooled all summer reverses refrigerant flow to heat the rooms. That reversing valve has stayed in the cooling position for months. The first cold night is a poor time to learn it will not move. A 15-minute test on a mild day answers the same question. The <a href="/services/heat-pump-repair-tampa/">heat pump repair</a> page is where that fault goes if the test fails.</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Set the thermostat to heat,</strong> a few degrees above the current room temperature.</li>
<li><strong>Wait about 15 minutes.</strong> Heat pumps do not blast hot air the way a furnace does.</li>
<li><strong>Check a supply vent</strong> in a bedroom, not the return grille.</li>
<li><strong>Go outside</strong> and confirm the outdoor unit is running, not just the indoor fan.</li>
<li><strong>Notice the smell</strong> after the first few minutes, then again at the end of the test.</li>
</ul>

<h2>What the smell and the supply air are telling you</h2>

<p>A short dusty smell, even a light burnt-dust smell, is common on the first run. Dust has been sitting on the electric heat strips since last winter. It should fade within a few minutes. A smell that stays, or any smell of hot plastic, means turn the system off.</p>

<p>Cool air at the supply vents while the thermostat is set to heat is a fault. The usual causes are a reversing valve that did not shift, a defrost problem, low refrigerant, or heat strips that never energized. Below about 35°F outdoors, the heat pump leans on those strips for backup. The strips use a lot of electricity, so leaving the thermostat on emergency heat for days will show up on the power bill. They are the backup, not the everyday setting.</p>

<h2>The filter is still full from summer</h2>

<p>Change the 1-inch filter before you trust a weak-cooling complaint in October. From May through September that filter was in the airstream almost every day. Pets, pollen, and drywall dust from a neighbor's renovation pack it faster than the date on the frame. A loaded filter cuts airflow, so the coil runs colder, the house takes longer to hit the setpoint, and the rooms feel damp even when the outdoor unit is fine.</p>

<p>In this climate, 30 to 60 days is the realistic interval, closer to 30 with pets or nearby construction. A clean filter will not fix a dead capacitor or a stuck reversing valve. It does remove the cheapest false alarm before you pay for a diagnostic. If the filter is clean and the supply air is still weak, the next look is the coil and the blower, which is a <a href="/services/ac-maintenance-tampa/">maintenance</a> visit rather than another filter swap.</p>
