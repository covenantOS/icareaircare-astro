<h2>Cool air on heat is a mode problem</h2>

<p>A heat pump that blows cold air in Wesley Chapel is usually still running. The fan is moving air. The outdoor unit may be on. The thermostat says heat. The supply register is cool anyway. That pattern is different from a system that is silent.</p>

<p>October at the Tampa station is still a cooling month: a normal high of 85.6°F and a low of 69.2°F (Florida Climate Center, 1991-2020). A 60-degree morning is enough for a house in Seven Oaks or Meadow Pointe to ask for heat, then the afternoon goes back to the 80s. The first heat call of the season is when a reversing valve that sat in cool mode all summer shows the fault.</p>

<p>In the United States, people search "heat pump blowing cold air" about 720 times a month. The count climbs in November and peaks in January. "Heat pump repair Wesley Chapel" is a different, much smaller search. This page answers the symptom. The repair page is <a href="/services/heat-pump-repair-tampa/">heat pump repair</a>.</p>

<h2>Three causes we separate on site</h2>

<p><strong>The thermostat is on emergency heat, or the strips never came on.</strong> Emergency heat skips the outdoor unit and runs the electric strips alone. If the strips are open, the fan still blows room air that feels cool. If the lockout is set too high, the strips come on while the outdoor air is still mild, and the outdoor unit never gets the call. Look at the thermostat screen before anyone opens a panel. Heat and emergency heat are not the same button.</p>

<p><strong>The reversing valve did not shift.</strong> That valve swaps the refrigerant so the indoor coil becomes the warm coil. When it sticks in cool mode, the system keeps cooling while the thermostat asks for heat. A short wait does not fix a stuck valve. The check is supply temperature in heat, then in cool, on the same visit. A heat rise of only a few degrees with the outdoor fan running points at the valve or the sensor.</p>

<p><strong>The outdoor unit is in defrost, or the coil is packed.</strong> Defrost temporarily sends cool air indoors while the outdoor coil clears ice. That should end in a few minutes. Leaves on the top grille, common after the first dry week along Curley Road, cut the airflow that heat mode uses. Clear the grille and run the test again. Ice that stays, or cool air that stays after defrost, is a control or a charge problem.</p>

<h2>AC blowing cold air on heat</h2>

<p>People also search "AC blowing cold air on heat." The register feels the same. The equipment is not always a heat pump. A straight-cool condenser plus electric strips makes heat only when those strips pull in. The outdoor fan stays off on a heat call. Cool supply air on that setup means the strips, the sequencer, or the thermostat call.</p>

<p>A heat pump is the other machine. On Heat, the outdoor fan should run and the indoor supply should warm up after a few minutes. In the United States, "AC blowing cold air on heat" is about 110 searches a month. It is a national count, not a Wesley Chapel count. The local repair page is still <a href="/services/heat-pump-repair-tampa/">heat pump repair</a>.</p>

<h2>Heat pump stuck in defrost</h2>

<p>Defrost is supposed to last a few minutes. The outdoor fan can stop, the reversing valve shifts, and the indoor supply feels cool while the coil clears. When the board never leaves defrost, that cool air stays. Ice that comes back the same morning, after the grille is clear, points at the defrost sensor or the board.</p>

<p>Leaves on the top of the unit along Curley Road will ice a coil that was fine in September. Clear the grille, set the mode to Heat, and wait 10 minutes. Cool air after that wait is a repair, not another bag of leaves.</p>

<h2>Reversing valve stuck in cooling</h2>

<p>The reversing valve is the part that swaps the coils so the indoor coil gets hot. A valve stuck in cooling keeps the house in AC while the thermostat says heat. Cool mode still works. Heat mode does not. The check is one number in each mode: supply temperature on heat, then supply temperature on cool, on the same visit.</p>

<p>A rise of only a few degrees with the outdoor fan running points at the valve or the outdoor sensor. Adding refrigerant from the sidewalk hides that. The written note should show both temperatures before anyone opens a valve.</p>

<h2>Emergency heat left on</h2>

<p>Emergency heat runs the strips and leaves the outdoor unit off. It is a test, not the setting for an October afternoon in Seven Oaks or Meadow Pointe. If the strips are open, the fan still moves room air and the register feels cool. If the strips work, the air gets hot and the electric bill climbs fast.</p>

<p>Put the thermostat back on Heat after the test. Aux heat on a mild morning can be the lockout set too high, so the strips come on before the heat pump gets the call. Below about 35°F the strips are backup. They are not the everyday heat for Wesley Chapel.</p>

<h2>What the homeowner can check first</h2>

<p>Set the mode to Heat, not Emergency Heat. Set the fan to Auto. Raise the setpoint a few degrees above the room so the system has a real call. Wait 10 minutes. If the outdoor fan is running and the supply air is still cool, stop there. Adding refrigerant from the sidewalk hides a valve that never shifted.</p>

<p>A dusty smell that fades in a few minutes is dust on the strips. A smell that stays is a reason to shut the system off. Below about 35°F the strips are backup. They are not the everyday heat setting for a Wesley Chapel afternoon in October.</p>
