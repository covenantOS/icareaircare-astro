<h2>No heat in November is a mode check</h2>

<p>A heat pump that will not heat in Wesley Chapel is usually still powered. The thermostat says heat. The fan may be moving air. The outdoor unit may be quiet, or it may be running while the rooms stay at the morning temperature. Those are two different faults.</p>

<p>November at the Tampa station is still mostly cooling: a normal high of 78.9°F, a low of 60.7°F, 178 cooling degree days, and 34 heating degree days (Florida Climate Center, 1991-2020). A house in Seven Oaks or Meadow Pointe can ask for heat at breakfast and cooling after lunch. <h2>What the outdoor unit is doing</h2>

<p><strong>The outdoor fan never starts.</strong> Emergency heat skips the outdoor unit and runs the strips alone. If the strips are open, the indoor fan moves room air and the house never warms. Look at the thermostat before anyone opens a panel. Heat and emergency heat are different settings. Aux on a 60-degree morning can also mean the lockout is set too high, so the strips get the call and the heat pump does not.</p>

<p><strong>The outdoor fan runs and the supply stays cool.</strong> That pattern is the reversing valve or a defrost cycle that did not finish. Wait 10 minutes with the mode on Heat and the fan on Auto. Cool air after that wait is the job already described in <a href="/blogs/heat-pump-blowing-cold-air-wesley-chapel/">heat pump blowing cold air</a>. A short wait does not fix a valve that stayed in cool mode all summer.</p>

<p>Leaves on the top grille along Curley Road cut the airflow heat mode needs. Clear the grille and run the test again. The written note should include supply temperature in heat, whether the outdoor fan ran, and whether the strips pulled in.
